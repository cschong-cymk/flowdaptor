Bunny learns sound: Google Antigravity vs Linyan.

Same theme, two engines. One made a polished animated explainer; the other made a cinematic, photorealistic short. The gap says a lot about which tool is ready for real video production.

October 2026 · Compare

We keep hearing that AI video is "all the same now." So we ran the same brief through two very different tools and posted both results unedited. The prompt was simple: a bunny learns about sound. The two interpretations could hardly be more different.

The videos

Google Antigravity
Linyan (Seedance)

What Antigravity built

Google Antigravity turned the brief into a stylized animated explainer. The frame is dominated by a cute lab-coated bunny, UI panels about pitch and frequency, and clean infographic motion. It feels like an ed-tech clip or a product onboarding video — bright, readable, and deliberately non-photographic.

That is a perfectly valid output. If your goal is to teach a concept quickly and charm a young audience, this is the obvious direction. The animation is on-brand for Google's recent AI demos: friendly, polished, and safe.

What Linyan built

Linyan, running on Seedance, interpreted the same theme as a cinematic, photorealistic short. The standout shot is a zen garden where sound takes physical form as ripples in sand — an image that communicates acoustics without a single label, character, or explanatory panel.

The difference isn't just aesthetic. The Linyan output uses real-world materials, natural lighting, and metaphor instead of annotation. It looks like something you could drop into a brand film, a documentary title sequence, or a gallery installation. The Antigravity output looks like something you drop into a classroom.

Why this matters for production

Both clips answer the prompt, but only one expands what you can do with video as a medium. Antigravity's result is a graphic — useful, legible, and bounded by its own cartoon logic. Linyan's result is a scene — grounded, textured, and open to interpretation.

For working directors, marketers, and filmmakers, that distinction is everything. A tool that can turn "bunny learns sound" into a meditative sand-ripple sequence is a tool that can handle ambiguity, tone, and visual metaphor. A tool that returns a cute mascot in a lab coat is a tool that handles the brief literally. There's room for both, but if you're producing anything that needs to feel shot rather than drawn, the choice is clear.

The honest caveats

This is one theme, one generation each, interpreted through two different interfaces. We did not run ten variations and pick the winner. We also did not use identical prompts word for word — the two tools expect different inputs — so this is a comparison of direction, not a controlled benchmark.

Antigravity may well produce photorealistic output on a different prompt. Linyan may return something flat on a different day. The point here is narrower: when both tools are asked to interpret the same loose creative idea, Linyan's Seedance pipeline produced the more interesting and production-ready result.
